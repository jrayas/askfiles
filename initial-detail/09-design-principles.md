# Design Principles

## Look
- Clean, minimal and **borderless**. Whitespace, alignment and type size create structure.
- Matches the user's Obsidian theme, with no imposed colour scheme. One accent at most.
- Large, readable choices. Generous spacing.
- Line icons of one consistent family. Black in light themes, white in dark, inherited from the theme. The padlock (open and closed) and gear are one matching set. No colour emoji anywhere.
- State is shown by shape, not by loud colour.

## Feel
- Smooth scrolling for long lists, contained within the list area.
- Short slide or fade between pages: deeper goes one way, back goes the other. Fast enough never to annoy on the tenth note of the day.
- Conversational wording, one question per page.
- The fast path is always one keystroke: "Save here" can be the default.

## Layout
- Cards in rows and columns, adapting to available width automatically.
- Choice of grid or list, columns and card size, with per-folder overrides.
- Consistent by default. Overrides only where they clearly help.

## Settings philosophy
- The default must look great without any configuration.
- Few settings in the gear panel, grouped simply. Finer controls under Advanced.
- A reset option so people can experiment safely.
- Each option lives in one place.

## Input
- Full keyboard support: arrows move across rows and columns, typing filters, Enter confirms, Escape goes back.
- Touch friendly, with large targets.
- Hover states on desktop.
- Works on desktop and mobile versions of Obsidian.

## Accessibility
- Sufficient contrast in both light and dark themes.
- Clear focus indication for keyboard users.
- Icons have text labels available to assistive tools.
- Motion can be reduced for people who prefer it.

## Tone of voice
Friendly, short, human. Never technical or file-system jargon on the question pages.
